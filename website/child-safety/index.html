Zero tolerance CSAE

FoodRats has zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE) and child sexual abuse material (CSAM). FoodRats is a closed-group meal-sharing app made by Sebastián Cardona. These standards explain what is prohibited, what we do when we find it, and how anyone can report a concern. They apply to the FoodRats Android and iOS apps and to all content shared through them.

What is prohibited

The following are strictly forbidden anywhere in FoodRats — in plate photos, profile photos, display names, meal descriptions, comments, crew names, and any other content:

  • Child sexual abuse material (CSAM) of any kind.
  • The sexualization of minors, or content that sexually exploits or endangers children.
  • Grooming, solicitation, or any attempt to make sexual contact with a minor.
  • Trafficking, advertising, or facilitating access to CSAE/CSAM.
  • Promoting, normalizing, or trivializing the sexual abuse of children.

FoodRats is not directed to children under 13 (or the minimum age of digital consent in your country) and we do not knowingly allow them to create accounts.

How to report a concern

You do not need to leave the app to report something. Anyone can report content or a user that may involve child safety:

  1. In FoodRats, open the meal, comment, or profile in question.
  2. Tap the ⋯ (more) menu and choose Report.
  3. Select Child safety as the reason and submit.

You can also block a user from the same menu to immediately stop seeing their content.

To report a child-safety concern directly, email hello@chsumiapps.com.

What we do about it

When we become aware of CSAE or CSAM through a report or our own review, we act promptly and in accordance with applicable law:

  • Remove the content from the app.
  • Disable the responsible account and the user's access to the service.
  • Preserve relevant evidence as permitted by law.
  • Report apparent CSAM to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) CyberTipline and/or the appropriate authorities in the relevant jurisdiction.

A first child-safety report on a piece of content hides it immediately, pending human review.

Legal compliance

We comply with child-safety laws applicable to our service, including obligations to report apparent CSAM. We cooperate with law enforcement and child-protection organizations as required by law.
